AirGlass · Windows 10/11 · Free & open source

Your iPhone screen, on your PC.

AirGlass turns a Windows PC into an AirPlay receiver. Open Control Centre on your iPhone, tap Screen Mirroring, pick your PC — and the screen appears in a window on your desktop. No cable, no account, nothing on the phone.

Up in under two minutes.

AirGlass handles the fiddly parts — it downloads its own engine, verifies it, and tells you when the firewall needs a click.

Install and start

Run the installer, open AirGlass, click Download engine (one time, ~80 MB, checksum-verified), then Start mirroring.

Same Wi-Fi

Make sure the iPhone and the PC are on the same network. AirPlay discovery happens over mDNS — no Apple Bonjour install needed.

Mirror

On the iPhone: Control Centre → Screen Mirroring → your PC's name (default "AirGlass"). To stop, click Stop or exit from the tray.

A control panel, not a science project.

The engine is the open-source UxPlay, built from unmodified upstream source in AirGlass's own CI. The app is the friendly window around it.

Picture

Honest limits

What it can't do, and what we haven't proven yet — said out loud, up front.

  • Early release, untested end-to-end. As of 19 Oct 2026 the app is unit-tested (16/16) and the engine passes a CI smoke test on a real Windows runner, but no physical iPhone↔Windows session has been run yet. Treat first use as a field test — the built-in log panel exists for exactly that.
  • View-only. No touch or keyboard control of the iPhone. Apple blocks input injection on non-jailbroken iOS; that's a platform rule, not an app bug.
  • DRM apps show a black window. Netflix and friends block AirPlay capture on the iPhone side — the same for every receiver.
  • Expect ~100–300 ms of latency and up to 1080p in practice. Quality depends on your Wi-Fi.
  • SmartScreen will warn on first run — the installer isn't code-signed. Click More info → Run anyway.

Get AirGlass

Windows 10 or 11. Per-user install, no admin rights, uninstallable from Settings → Apps. The first release is being packaged now — the button takes you to the releases page, where the installer appears the moment it's published.
